# Crux Video Debate — Participant Rules and Format

**Version:** 1.0

**Last updated:** 2026-08-05

This is the complete participant-facing format for a Crux video debate. The host sends this
document to the FOR and AGAINST debaters before recording.

## 1. What you are joining

A Crux video debate is one motion argued through five different domain lenses. One participant
argues FOR the motion and one argues AGAINST it. A human host runs the recording, and an AI judge
rules the five rounds after the recording has been edited and transcribed.

The finished debate is published as an on-demand video. It is not live, and its result does not
change your Crux logic, arena record, season score or leaderboard position.

## 2. What both debaters receive

Before recording, both debaters receive the same:

- exact motion;
- five distinct domains and their round order;
- assigned side: FOR or AGAINST;
- recording date, time and joining instructions;
- technical preparation checklist.

The motion, sides and five domains do not change after they are issued.

## 3. Preparation rules

- You may research the motion and all five domains before recording.
- You may prepare concise written notes and keep them beside you during recording.
- Once recording begins, you may not use live web search, an AI assistant, messages from another
  person or off-camera coaching.
- Your argument must be your own spoken response. Pre-recorded answers are not permitted.
- Formal citations are not required, but you are responsible for factual claims. Name the source
  when relying on a precise statistic, study or quotation.
- A fabricated or materially unsupported claim may weaken the AI judge's ruling.

The same preparation rules apply to both sides.

## 4. Complete debate order

### Introduction

The host introduces the participants, motion and format. The introduction is not judged.

### Opening-order draw

A recorded coin toss decides which side opens Round 1. The opening side then alternates:

| Round | Opening side |
|---|---|
| 1 | Coin-toss winner |
| 2 | Other side |
| 3 | Coin-toss winner |
| 4 | Other side |
| 5 | Coin-toss winner |

### Five rounds

Each round uses one of the five announced domains and always has this order:

1. Opening side — 30 judged seconds.
2. Responding side — 30 judged seconds.
3. Grace discussion — variable length and completely unjudged.
4. Host closes the round and announces the next domain.

All five rounds are recorded and judged even if one side has already won three rounds.

### Outro

The host closes the debate. The outro is not judged.

## 5. The 30-second judged statement

- The host clearly starts each timed statement.
- The timer runs for 30 seconds from the host's start signal.
- Only words inside that 30-second window are sent to the round judge.
- Words spoken after the cutoff belong to grace and do not count toward the ruling.
- Finish your argument inside the timer; there is no automatic “finish the sentence” extension.
- The other debater must not interrupt a judged statement.
- Each round is judged on its assigned domain. A strong point from another domain does not replace
  the need to answer the current lens.

The participant speaking second may rebut the first statement, but speaking second is not itself
a scoring advantage. Opening order alternates to distribute that position across the match.

## 6. Grace discussion

Grace begins after both judged statements and ends when the host closes it.

- The host decides its length based on the conversation and the programme's ten-minute maximum.
- All three participants may speak.
- Clarification, rebuttal and natural conversation are welcome.
- Nothing said during grace affects the round winner, judge split or extracted judged points.
- Grace remains visible and audible in the published video, so the conduct rules still apply.
- The host may interrupt, redirect or end grace to protect time and order.

Do not hold an essential argument for grace. If you want it judged, say it inside your 30 seconds.

## 7. How the AI judge rules

The judge receives only the two 30-second statements from the current round. It does not receive
the introduction, host speech, grace, outro or future rounds.

The judge compares the statements in this order:

1. relevance to the assigned domain;
2. logical soundness and support;
3. handling of the motion's burden within that domain;
4. specificity and material significance.

The judge must not reward or punish accent, grammar, fluency, polish, statement length or speaking
order.

Every round produces:

- exactly one winner: FOR or AGAINST;
- an explanatory split such as `FOR 62 — 38 AGAINST`;
- a short ruling;
- up to four key points per side, linked to the moment each point was spoken.

A round cannot draw. The split explains the ruling; it is not a scientific probability and is not
added across rounds.

After all five rounds, the side with more round wins wins the debate. Because there are five
rounds and no round draws, the match cannot draw.

## 8. Published viewing experience

The published page reveals the debate progressively:

- transcript and key points appear as they are spoken;
- a round ruling appears only when that round ends;
- the final verdict appears only after Round 5 ends;
- seeking backward hides later analysis, and seeking forward reveals the destination state.

The host provides the programme audio. The website displays three synchronized participant video
angles and emphasizes the current speaker.

## 9. Conduct

- Challenge the argument, evidence or reasoning—not the person.
- Personal insults, harassment, threats, slurs and discriminatory abuse are not permitted.
- Do not knowingly fabricate evidence or misrepresent a source.
- Follow the host's timing and moderation instructions.
- Do not speak over the other debater's judged statement.

The host may pause or stop recording when conduct or a technical problem makes the format unfair.

## 10. Retakes and technical failures

- A segment may be restarted when audio, video, connection failure or host error prevented a fair
  recording.
- A weak answer, forgotten point or regret after hearing the opponent is not a reason for a
  retake.
- If a substantive retake is unavoidable after both statements were heard, the entire round is
  re-recorded from its opening statement, with the same domain and opening order.
- Both debaters are told which take will be used.
- The host's timing and technical ruling is final for the recording session.

## 11. Recording and publication consent

By joining the recording, each participant confirms that they understand:

- the session will be recorded and edited;
- the finished debate, transcript, AI-extracted points and AI verdict may be published publicly;
- editing may remove dead time and technical interruptions but may not change the meaning or order
  of a judged statement;
- the result is editorial and unranked;
- corrections to obvious transcription errors may be made before judging without rewriting the
  participant's argument.

This format acknowledgment does not replace any separate recording or publication release the
organizer requires. The organizer provides and collects that release independently when needed.

## 12. Participant checklist

Before joining, confirm that you have:

- read the motion, five domains and assigned side;
- researched and prepared any notes you need;
- closed live search, AI and messaging tools;
- connected headphones and a stable microphone;
- tested camera, lighting and internet connection;
- reserved the full session time stated by the host—the published edit is capped at ten minutes,
  but setup, technical checks and retakes can make the recording session longer;
- read and accepted the timing, conduct, retake and publication rules above.
